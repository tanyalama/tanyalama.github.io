Scientific reasoning includes generating and testing hypotheses and interpreting results. It also includes the transferable skills of collaboration and communication. Most students will not become scientists. Students who can pose questions and draw conclusions from evidence, however, become informed and scientifically literate members of their communities.

## Approach

**Process over static knowledge.** I pair process-based thinking with content mastery through guided-inquiry activities. In Genomics, students work through each element of the computational pipeline and build a detailed conceptual diagram over the semester. Emphasizing process deepens their grasp of core concepts such as natural selection and mutation. It also shows them that a null result is still valuable when it rests on sound reasoning. Students named learning "how to think in a process-oriented way" as one of the top skills they gained.

**Engaged, inclusive learning in larger classes.** In lecture courses I use collaborative active-learning strategies: "One Sentence Synthesis" summaries of key concepts, co-created diagrams, and shared definitions for abstract ideas such as "gene." With the Spinelli Center, I am setting up weekend peer tutoring sessions built around the Challenge of the Week.
